    Recommendation

    Build Horizon. Borrow one idea from each of the others.

    Horizon answers the question people open the app for, first and largest, and its grouped rows grow cleanly as the ship gains capabilities. The other two each solve a problem Horizon will meet soon.

    1. Horizon is the home screenScene, ETA, Fuel and Cargo rows, one contextual action. Systems is a third row when the height allows and lives in the report when it doesn't. The report sheet replaces the old Cargo tab.
    2. Logbook's sentence for hard statesWhen data is stale, arrival is being checked, or a command is unresolved, swap the big number for one plain sentence.
    3. Voyage's trip bar for the fleetEach ship in the future fleet sheet becomes a trip-bar row: sprite, status, destination, ETA.

    Drop the tab bar for now. With one ship, Bridge, Cargo and Activity were mostly empty rooms. The report covers cargo and systems; recent activity can be a single row until it earns a place.

    Second pass

    Fit the phone you actually have.

    Pass one looked right on a tall phone and failed on a short browser window. Pass two made these changes.

    1. 1
      Horizon sizes itself from the height it measures, not the device. On a 375 × 548 browser the ship name, ETA, Fuel, Cargo and the action all fit without scrolling. Large text still scrolls.
    2. 2
      Previews now open with just two controls: a link back to the report and a Preview controls sheet holding state, pause, reduced motion and restart. Demo controls stay outside the app.
    3. 3
      Logbook lists Fuel and Cargo above the journey, so the scrolling part is the story, not the essentials.
    4. 4
      The folded data is now called Selected API fields. Values keep their types, unknowns show as null, and explanations sit in notes beside the values instead of inside them.

    Grouping follows Apple's guidance on layout (alignment, space and separators before containers) and sheets (Done closes, Back returns).

    Kept from the first board

    The cute ship never lies.

    1. 1The countdown comes from the arrival instant. At zero it says “Checking”. Nothing unlocks from a client timer.
    2. 2Ship position on any route is drawn from departure and arrival times. It is never presented as telemetry.
    3. 3While in transit, the API's waypoint symbol is the destination, not the current location.
    4. 4Times display locally with a zone. Raw ISO instants stay available in the report.
    5. 5Pause freezes the picture only. Deadlines never move.

    Design study. Every ship, value, time and command on this page is a demo. No requests are sent.
