Bridge

Bridge turns the Campaign page from a full status readout into a short report you can read in ten seconds. Your chief of staff tells you how the plan is going, what the fleet is doing, and anything that has stopped and needs you, with the button to deal with it. Primary target: iPhone 17 Pro (402 × 874 pt), in Safari or as a Home Screen web app. Secondary: iPad Pro 13" M4 (1376 × 1032 pt landscape, 1032 × 1376 portrait). Ship names, routes and numbers below are sample data. The 11 decisions behind this version are in bridge-ui-decisions.md, and the engineering plan is in bridge-ui-plan.md.

What the screen is for

On naming and voice. The screen is called Bridge. An XO is a single ship's second in command. The officer who briefs a fleet's commander is the chief of staff, and the commander of a fleet is an admiral. Bridge speaks as that chief of staff, in character, in one place only: the summary sentence under the credits box ("Admiral, 1 item needs your attention."). Card titles, row text, numbers, buttons and errors stay plain and literal, and "Admiral" never appears in a button or a screen-reader label. The home tab is still called Brief.

Ranking, highest first, from your request and the way short military briefs are written (the key number and bottom line up front, then only the exceptions). The credits box always sits directly under the page's title line (“Bridge · Day 2 of 7 · Earn phase”), in the same place on every screen, tab and state (other screens show a smaller credits bar there). The agent switcher pill sits in the top bar above it:

  1. Credits box. Credits now (largest), change last hour and this wipe, then ships, routes running and time to the reset in the same box.
  2. Bottom line. One sentence under the box, in the chief of staff's voice: does anything need you, and is the fleet earning? The same facts set the status pill and the screen's main button.
  3. Needs you. Only failures the plan can't handle itself: a ship, the main campaign, a whole agent's fleet or the engine has stopped. Each one says what happened, what it stopped, the evidence, and offers the button that resolves it. Buying ships and other plan choices are the plan's job; they show up in the log, never as questions. A kill-switch drain counts here while its affected ships finish settling cargo. Warnings that need no action sit in one quiet "Also watching" row. Nothing needs you? The section says so in one line.
  4. Operations. Each running route (good + where it's bought + where it's sold) with its ships and earnings rate. Probes are summed into one row. Mining appears only once an approved playbook mines.
  5. Plan. Phase (discover → earn → wind down), the link to “How C0 decides” (the Strategy page) and one compact request-budget line. On iPad, a chart of actual credits over the wipe. There is no plan-pace line in v1.
  6. Log. The last three notable events, then a link to the full log.

Today's detailed ship cards, decision rejections and load tables move to the Routes, Fleet and Log tabs and to the ship report that already exists on the flight deck. Nothing is lost; it just stops being the first thing you see.

Conventions:

  • One main button per screen, taken from the most urgent item.
  • Touch targets are at least 44 × 44 pt, and layout insets come from env(safe-area-inset-*), never hard-coded, so Safari and the Home Screen app both fit.
  • Severity is shown by word, position and colour, never colour alone.
  • Numbers are tabular. Money is short (4.81M), with the exact figure for screen readers and on tap.
  • Local times carry the zone (PST) next to relative ones.
  • Ships show their display name with the callsign beside it (Light Hauler #9 GIANTSOUP-9).
  • An “Updated” time sits inside the credits box and becomes a stale notice if updates stop, so nothing ever sits between the title line and the credits.
  • Pause and resume always confirm in a sheet that names exactly what will happen, and to which scope: one ship, the main campaign or the whole fleet.
  • The theme is always dark. Text wraps at large text sizes instead of being cut off.

Final design · Brief home with four tabs

The frames are static illustrations. Use the section links above to move between them.

The Brief is the home screen. It opens with the credits box, then the chief of staff's one-sentence summary, anything that has stopped and needs you, and short summaries of routes, ships, the plan and the log. Four tabs run along the bottom, Brief · Routes · Fleet · Log, and lead to the detail: every row in a list opens a page for that route or ship.

  • Credits always come first. The Brief has the full credits box. Every other screen has a smaller credits bar in the same place, under the “Bridge · Day 2 of 7 · Earn phase” title line. Both end with the time of the last update. If updates stop, that line turns amber and reads “Not updated since 4:12 PM PST — reconnecting”.
  • One Brief per agent. The pill in the top bar names the agent you're looking at (GIANTSOUP). When another agent has something stopped, the pill carries a red badge with the number of such agents and the words “1 other agent needs you” for screen readers. Tapping it lists the agents; picking one keeps you on the same tab.
  • Only failures ask for you. A card appears under “Needs you” when a ship, the main campaign, an agent’s fleet or the engine has stopped its normal work. A drain still settling cargo counts here. Its button opens a review sheet and is the screen's one main button. The Brief is arranged so that the first card and its button show above the tab bar without scrolling. Probe purchases, completed loads and playbook milestones go in the log.
  • From the phone you can resume one stopped ship, resume the main campaign or fleet, pause the fleet, and retire a ship past its loss limit. Each review sheet lists its exact targets. Phone Retire for expired approvals or five failed decisions awaits Taylor’s confirmation. Playbook approvals, arm creation, loss-limit changes and renewals stay on the PC.
  • Every row reads the same way. The name is on the first line (display name, callsign beside it), status in words on the second, and one number on the right (earning rate, arrival time or cargo). Stopped items sort to the top. Group rows, such as Ended routes and Probes, show their count on the second line and open a list.
  • Moving around. Tapping a row opens its page inside the same tab, and Back returns to the screen you came from (a route opened from a ship's page says “‹ Light Hauler #8”). “Routes ›”, “Fleet ›” and “Log ›” on the Brief switch to that tab. “How C0 decides ›” on the Plan card opens the Strategy page, which offers “‹ Bridge” to come back. Tapping the tab you're on goes back to its list. On a route page, “All 41 ›” switches to the Log tab filtered to that route’s completed loads. The Brief tab's badge counts the items that need you on this agent.
  • No campaign authorized. Show engine health, “Admiral, no campaign is currently authorized” and instructions to run campaign:approve on the PC. While an authorized agent is between wipes, show setup status and engine health with no archived credits or log.
  • Other Brief states. Three screens from the earlier concepts still apply to the Brief, with the tab bar, the agent pill and the final credits box (“Updated” time inside it), and without concept A's pinned bottom button. The main button sits on the card: Review Light Hauler #9 in H1, Review balance on A4's Fleet paused card. The screens are the review sheet (A3), the all-clear Brief (A2) and the fleet-stopped Brief (A4). Their copy follows the voice guide in the plan (for example, all clear reads “Admiral, all 12 ships are working. We're earning 290k an hour.”).
  • Route and ship pages are for reading. Their only button is Review, on something that has stopped. The ship page shows the ship's illustration (the existing ShipScene). The full ship report on the flight deck is one link away.
  • iPad. The tabs sit at the top. Routes and Fleet show the list on the left and the selected route or ship on the right. Tapping a route or ship on the Brief opens that tab with it selected.
  • Rollout. Bridge first ships beside today's page at /campaign/bridge, with a link each way. After it has proven itself on a live wipe, it replaces /campaign and becomes the page you land on after logging in.
All of this is New work: the tab bar, the agent pill, the pages, the route and ship pages, their URLs, the iPad split view, the four phone actions and the queries behind them. Data available today lists what already exists; bridge-ui-plan.md has the build plan.

iPad Pro 13"

Shown at 50%. On iPad the four tabs sit centred at the top, as in iPadOS apps; the agent pill and Menu sit on the right. The Brief uses two columns. Routes and Fleet become split views: the list on the left and the selected route or ship on the right, so opening one never hides the list.

Attention catalogue

The ways the engine stops or warns as of main on 2026-11-23, how each would read as a card, and its button. Only Stopped items count toward “Needs you” and the badges. A Stopped item means a ship, the main campaign, an agent's whole fleet or the engine is not working, and is shown in red. Watching items are warnings while work continues, shown in grey, and collapse into one “Also watching” row. Recorded plan milestones, such as buying a probe or “new playbook queued for the next safe point”, go to the log and the Plan card. They never ask you anything.

Terms used below:

  • The main campaign is the command ship's approved weekly playbook (C0).
  • Each extra trade ship runs under its own approval, called an arm. On screen it is just “the ship”.
  • The whole fleet means one agent's main campaign and all its live arms.
  • Several agents (GIANTSOUP, SOUPFLEET, …) can each run a main campaign.

Status tags:

  • Exists: the data or action exists on main.
  • PC only: it stays on the command line on the PC by decision.
  • New: Bridge adds it.
  • Later: it needs engine work outside Bridge.

Every resume goes through a sheet that states what Resume will do, because the engine's rule is that resuming needs operator review and never replays an old preview. The server checks the selected agent, reset and exact grant set. At the stored episode reset boundary, resume and retire wait for confirmation of the current wipe. A later extension in the cache does not override the episode boundary. A changed resume or retire target requires a fresh review. Bridge records outstanding arm results in campaign → agent → result lock order before the SQLite authority transaction. Any newly discovered loss remains visible on the Brief and in the Retire review even when Resume is refused. Fleet pause covers the current eligible set and reports changes. Its feedback distinguishes stop requested from settled and names the review blocker if a run still holds its active slot. Whole-fleet Pause stays reachable from the bottom of the Brief and from Menu, but is never the screen's main button.

CampaignHealth emits stall warnings when the engine is alive and the primary is unpaused. Today it and the watchdog read only main campaigns, so an arm pause sends no notification. Bridge shows arm pauses on screen. Arm notifications are an engine follow-up.

Decisions recorded (2026-11-23)

Taylor answered the six original questions and five follow-ups on 2026-11-23. Taylor confirmed the three remaining implementation choices. The full wording, answers and consequences are in bridge-ui-decisions.md. The build plan is in bridge-ui-plan.md. Already decided on 2026-11-17: the Brief is the home screen, there are four tabs, and the plan buys ships on its own, so you're asked only about failures.

  1. Voice and name. The screen is called Bridge and speaks in character, as your chief of staff (“Admiral, 1 item needs your attention”). The character stays in the summary sentence; everything you act on stays plain.
  2. One ship from the phone. Resume a single stopped ship through a review sheet. Retire is available when recorded loss exceeds the approved limit (net < −max_net_loss), after five director failures, or after the approval expires. Retiring a draining arm stops its sell_aboard recovery. Playbook approvals, renewals and loss-limit changes stay on the PC.
  3. Plan pace. Not in v1. The chart shows actual credits only. Playbook target curves come later.
  4. Routes. A route is a distinct good + source + buyer. Probes are counted separately. Mining is hidden until a mining playbook exists.
  5. Home. Bridge becomes the operator's post-login home, switched at cutover, not during the trial.
  6. Safari and Home Screen. Both are supported: a minimal web manifest plus safe-area insets everywhere.
  7. Several agents. One Brief per agent, with an agent pill at the top that carries a badge when another agent needs you.
  8. Strategy. Linked from the Plan card (“How C0 decides ›”), keeping four clean tabs.
  9. Request budget and kill switch. Exceptions only. A kill-switch drain is a Needs-you card; high deferrals or 429s are Also watching; one compact line sits under Plan.
  10. Ship names and pictures. Display names with the callsign beside them. Rows are compact text; the ship page shows ShipScene.
  11. Rollout. Bridge ships beside the current page at /campaign/bridge, with a link each way, through a live wipe. Then it replaces /campaign and becomes the post-login home.

Confirmed implementation decisions

  1. Block resume of a drained agent while any applicable kill-switch condition is active.
  2. Allow phone Retire for a loss stop, director_error after five failures, and an expired approval.
  3. Keep command-ship stalls at Watching in Bridge v1 and defer the 45-minute escalation to Stopped to a separate engine task.
Earlier concepts, for reference

These are the options drawn before you chose the Brief with four tabs. They are kept for the record and are not the design to build, except A2, A3 and A4, which the final design reuses with its tab bar, agent pill and credits box and without the pinned bottom button (on A4, Review balance sits on the card). They were drawn before the 2026-11-23 decisions, so they still say “Fleet brief”, use plain wording and callsigns instead of display names, and show mining and a plan-pace line. Their sample numbers may also differ slightly from the final design above. Where they differ, the final design and bridge-ui-decisions.md are right.

A · Brief

One scrolling page in report order. The first screen answers "what's the number, are we OK, what needs me", in that order, without scrolling; everything after it is supporting detail. On the phone it uses the app's compact header with a Menu button, as the flight deck already does. Today /campaign uses the full header with inline links, so switching it to the compact header is a small layout change, not new navigation.

B · Inbox first

The credits box stays on top, then the page becomes a to-do list, with Operations and Log behind a segmented control and no summary sentence. Better if the queue is often long (for example, several ships failing at once in a 100-ship wipe). Worse at answering "how is the plan going" at a glance, because the trend and routes are one tap away.

C · Tab bar

The Brief from concept A becomes one of four tabs: Brief, Routes, Fleet, Log. The design brief says to add bottom navigation only when there are distinct, useful top-level destinations. With 50+ ships that becomes true, so this is the growth path for A rather than a rival to it. The Brief tab carries a badge with the number of open items.
