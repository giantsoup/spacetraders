Campaign screen redesign · final design · 2026-11-17

Fleet brief

Turn the Campaign page from a full status readout into a short report you can read in ten seconds: how the plan is going, what the fleet is doing, and anything that has stopped and needs you, with the button to deal with it. Primary target: iPhone 17 Pro (402 × 874 pt). Secondary: iPad Pro 13" M4 (1376 × 1032 pt landscape, 1032 × 1376 portrait). Ship names, routes and numbers below are sample data.

What the screen is for

On naming. An XO is a single ship's second in command. In a navy, the officer who briefs a fleet's commander is the chief of staff, and the commander of a fleet is an admiral (a commodore for a small squadron). The screen doesn't need the role-play to work, so this design calls it the Fleet brief and writes in plain language. Wording in character ("Admiral, two items need you") is an easy later choice; see the questions at the end.

Ranking, highest first, from your request and the way short military briefs are written (the key number and bottom line up front, then only the exceptions). The credits box always sits directly under the page's title line (“Fleet brief · Day 2 of 7 · Earn phase”), in the same place on every screen, tab and state (other screens show a smaller credits bar there):

  1. Credits box. Credits now (largest), change last hour and this wipe, then ships, routes running (trade and mining) and time to the reset in the same box.
  2. Bottom line. One sentence under the box: is the fleet earning, and does anything need you? The same words set the status pill and the screen's main button.
  3. Needs you. Only failures the plan can't handle itself: a route, a ship or the whole fleet has stopped. Each one says what happened, what it stopped, the evidence, and offers the button that resolves it. Buying ships and other plan choices are the plan's job; they show up in the log, never as questions. Warnings that need no action sit in one quiet "Also watching" row. Nothing needs you? The section says so in one line.
  4. Operations. Each running route with its ships and earnings rate. Probes and scouts are summed into one row.
  5. Plan. Phase (discover → earn → wind down) and pace toward the goal: one line on the phone, a credit chart on iPad.
  6. Log. The last three notable events, then a link to the full log.

Today's detailed ship cards, decision rejections and load tables move to the Routes, Fleet and Log tabs and to the ship report that already exists on the flight deck. Nothing is lost; it just stops being the first thing you see.

Conventions: one main button per screen, taken from the most urgent item; touch targets at least 44 × 44 pt; layout insets from env(safe-area-inset-*), never hard-coded; severity shown by word, position and colour, never colour alone; tabular numbers; short money (4.81M) with the exact figure for screen readers and on tap; local times with the zone (PST) next to relative ones; an “Updated” time inside the credits box that becomes a stale notice if updates stop, so nothing ever sits between the title line and the credits; pause and resume always confirm in a sheet that says exactly what will happen; dark theme always; text wraps at large text sizes instead of being cut off.

Final design · Brief home with four tabs

The Brief is the home screen. It opens with the credits box, then one sentence on how things are going, anything that has stopped and needs you, and short summaries of routes, ships, the plan and the log. Four tabs along the bottom, Brief · Routes · Fleet · Log, lead to the detail: every row in a list opens a page for that route or ship.

  • Credits always come first. The Brief has the full credits box. Every other screen has a smaller credits bar in the same place, under the “Fleet brief · Day 2 of 7 · Earn phase” title line. Both end with the time of the last update. If updates stop, that line turns amber and reads “Not updated since 4:12 PM PST — reconnecting”.
  • Only failures ask for you. A card appears under “Needs you” only when a ship, a route or the whole fleet has stopped. Its button opens a review sheet and is the screen's one main button. The Brief is arranged so the first card and its button show above the tab bar without scrolling. Buying ships and other plan choices go in the log; they never ask you anything.
  • Every row reads the same way. Name on the first line, status in words on the second, one number on the right (earning rate, arrival time or cargo). Stopped items sort to the top. Group rows, such as Ended routes and Probes, show their count on the second line and open a list.
  • Moving around. Tapping a row opens its page inside the same tab, and Back returns to the screen you came from (a route opened from a ship's page says “‹ GIANTSOUP-8”). “Routes ›”, “Fleet ›” and “Log ›” on the Brief switch to that tab. Tapping the tab you're on goes back to its list. On a route page, “All 41 ›” opens its full load list. The Brief tab's badge counts the items that need you.
  • Other Brief states. Three screens from the earlier concepts still apply to the Brief, with the tab bar and the final credits box (“Updated” time inside it) and without concept A's pinned bottom button. The main button sits on the card: Review GIANTSOUP-9 in H1, Review balance on A4's Fleet paused card. The screens: the review sheet (A3), the all-clear Brief (A2) and the fleet-stopped Brief (A4).
  • Route and ship pages are for reading. Their only button is Review, on something that has stopped. The full ship report on the flight deck is one link away.
  • iPad. The tabs sit at the top. Routes and Fleet show the list on the left and the selected route or ship on the right; tapping a route or ship on the Brief opens that tab with it selected.
All of this is New work: the tab bar, the four pages, the route and ship pages, their URLs, the iPad split view and the queries behind them. Data available today lists what already exists.

iPad Pro 13"

Attention catalogue

The ways the engine stops or warns today, how it would read as a card, and its button. Only Stopped items (a route, a ship or the whole fleet is not working, red) count toward “Needs you”. Watching items (a warning; work continues, grey) collapse into one “Also watching” row. Plan choices, such as buying ships, and plain notices, such as “replacement playbook waiting for a safe point”, go to the log and the Plan section. They never ask you anything.

Two terms used below: the main campaign is the command ship's approved weekly playbook; each extra trade ship runs under its own approval, called an arm.

Questions for you

Decided: the Brief is the home screen; four tabs, Brief · Routes · Fleet · Log; the plan buys ships on its own, and you're asked only about failures.

  1. Voice. Plain ("1 item needs you") or in character ("Admiral, one item needs your attention")? And the name: Fleet brief, Bridge, or something else? (The menu link reads “Campaign” today.)
  2. Resume one ship from the phone. Resuming a single stopped ship is CLI-only today. Should the phone get that button (after the review sheet)? This design keeps approvals, renewals and loss limits off the phone: the plan renews ships, and a new playbook is approved on the PC during the weekly review.
  3. Goal pace. Do you want the "plan pace" line? It needs a target curve, e.g. the 8B model's planning scenario, stored with each playbook.
  4. What counts as a route. A distinct good + source + buyer (or mining site) with at least one ship on it right now? Probes counted separately, as drawn? No campaign playbook mines today, so should the mining count include manual mining runs, or wait until a playbook adds mining?
  5. Home. Should the brief replace /campaign only, or also become the first page after you log in (today that's Agents)?
  6. Safari or Home Screen. Do you open it in Safari or as a Home Screen web app? A Home Screen app gets the full 874 pt and no browser bar, and would need a small web manifest.
Earlier concepts, for reference

These are the options drawn before you chose the Brief with four tabs. They are kept for the record and are not the design to build, except A2, A3 and A4, which the final design reuses with its tab bar and credits box and without the pinned bottom button (on A4, Review balance sits on the card). Their sample numbers were drawn first and may differ slightly from the final design above. Where they differ, the final design is right.

A · Brief

One scrolling page in report order. The first screen answers "what's the number, are we OK, what needs me", in that order, without scrolling; everything after it is supporting detail. On the phone it uses the app's compact header with a Menu button, as the flight deck already does. Today /campaign uses the full header with inline links, so switching it to the compact header is a small layout change, not new navigation.

B · Inbox first

The credits box stays on top, then the page becomes a to-do list, with Operations and Log behind a segmented control and no summary sentence. Better if the queue is often long (for example, several ships failing at once in a 100-ship wipe). Worse at answering "how is the plan going" at a glance, because the trend and routes are one tap away.

C · Tab bar

The Brief from concept A becomes one of four tabs: Brief, Routes, Fleet, Log. The design brief says to add bottom navigation only when there are distinct, useful top-level destinations. With 50+ ships that becomes true, so this is the growth path for A rather than a rival to it. The Brief tab carries a badge with the number of open items.

iPad Pro 13" (M4), first layouts

Shown at 50% of real size. The iPad doesn't stretch the phone layout: the brief and queue stay in a phone-width left column, and the right side uses the extra width for tables and the credit chart. In Split View or Stage Manager windows narrower than about 700 pt, the page falls back to the phone layout.
