Recommended policy

Start earning before you buy a strategy

My default is a short discovery pass followed by one small trade using the existing command ship, if fresh prices and a safe route support it. October–November 2026 operating notes make trading the first hypothesis to test. Compare it with a complete mining-and-sale trip on an existing capable ship. A profitable, reachable starter contract can take priority. [17] [20]

1. DiscoverActual fleet, fuel, buyers, contract terms.
2. Prove a loopMining, trade, or a contract. Record full costs.
3. ExpandHauling, extraction, information, or routes.
4. Collect returnsStop buying when payback exceeds the cycle.

If no trade has fresh prices at both ends and a margin after costs, use mining as the fallback when equipment, buyer, and fuel allow it. Do not buy a drone or hauler just to imitate a guide. If neither loop clears the reserve and recovery requirements, gather the specific missing evidence before spending.

Why this choice: one recent bot reports stronger realized command-ship trading than its mining baseline, but results depend on its world, software, and uptime. Mining avoids purchased inventory and remains a useful comparison. No public source establishes which wins in your next starting system. The first completed trips give a provisional choice; repeated results decide expansion.

Mechanics that change the strategy

Official mechanics

Prices need a ship

Market directories expose listed goods. Prices require a ship at that waypoint. An orbiting probe can provide visibility, so docking a trader is not the only way to gather prices. Unknown prices are not zero. [7] [8]

Official mechanics

Volume limits each trade

tradeVolume caps units bought or sold per transaction. Larger holds can require several transactions at changing prices. A one-batch quote is not a full-hold sale guarantee. [8]

Official mechanics

Exports depend on imports

Official market guidance links export production to adequate imports. Supplying an input is a candidate for durable trade, but its delayed benefit is shared with other players. Do not assume fixed replenishment or profit. [7]

Older instructions can mislead even within v2. Surveys should use the dedicated POST /my/ships/{shipSymbol}/extract/survey operation with the full unmodified signed survey. The older survey property on ordinary extraction is deprecated. [8]

After registration / first productive session

Opening: discover enough, then finish one job

  1. Confirm the world and fleet. After the reset is confirmed, register or import a current agent through the app. Refresh agent, all ships, cargo, fuel, equipment, cooldowns, and contracts. Starting ships and credits are inputs to discover, not constants to script. [8]
  2. Cap initial discovery at 10–15 minutes of active work. List the home system's waypoints with limit=20 and follow every page. Mark mining candidates, known fuel sellers, markets, and shipyards. Keep hidden traits as unknown. Fetch goods directories to narrow likely buyers before flying. If discovery overruns, pursue the best already-feasible job.
  3. Protect recovery money. As a starting policy, reserve the greater of 20,000 CR or the quoted cost of reaching and refueling at a known safe market. Keep cargo working capital separate. The app's default manual floor is 2,000 CR; that is a software minimum, not this plan's operating budget. [12]
  4. Use existing price coverage early. Check an existing probe’s equipment and ability to move before assigning a destination. A stationary probe may already cover a useful market. Working ships should log prices on normal routes. Buy selective coverage when stale information is demonstrably costing more than its setup cost; do not copy SPECTER’s 26-probe purchase. [17]
  5. Pick the first loop from actual terms. A miner needs installed mining equipment, required processing capability, free cargo, an extractable site, a reachable buyer, and a fuel-safe route. A trader needs fresh source and destination prices, cargo capacity, and working capital. A contract needs feasible sourcing, delivery, and a deadline you can meet. [8] [9]
  6. Complete and reconcile the loop. Include outbound travel, cooldowns, all cargo disposal or sales, return travel, and fuel restoration. Record confirmed transaction totals and final credits. Repeat once before treating the route as the default.

Value contracts without counting the advance twice

Before acceptance, compare total acceptance and fulfillment payments with sourcing costs, fuel, other costs, and the income displaced during the job. For mined delivery goods, include their alternative sale value. Check every delivery line and both acceptance and fulfillment deadlines. [8]

For an already accepted contract, the advance is historical cash. Decide whether finishing is worthwhile using remaining payments, remaining obligations, and the actual consequences of non-completion. Do not assume a loss unlocks a better next contract.

Compare full cycles instead of headline spreads

For mining, log the value of every yielded good at reachable buyers. A closer asteroid can still be worse after mixed yields and sale prices. The July 2026 planner discussion identifies exactly this weakness in flat revenue estimates. [14]

Realized net CR = confirmed receipts − cargo purchases − fuel consumed at replacement cost − repairs and other operating costs.
Net CR per elapsed hour = realized net CR ÷ full cycle hours.

For a provisional trade batch, use the minimum of free cargo, source trade volume, destination trade volume, affordable units after the reserve, and the test budget. Calculate units × (destination sellPrice − source purchasePrice), then subtract full-route costs. Refresh after each batch. This is an estimate until the server confirms both transactions.

Compare strategies from similar starting cargo and fuel states. Keep ship purchases separate from operating profit. Count unsold cargo as inventory, not realized income. For multi-ship setups, judge the added fleet income against the best use of those same ships.

Later in the cycle / goals and reset horizon

Late game: deepen the winning route, then collect cash

Default goal: cash generation

Expand routes that remain profitable as volume rises. Spread sales across verified buyers where that improves realized returns. Keep enough API capacity for work and recovery. More ships do not help if sale demand or request scheduling is already saturated.

Alternative: exploration

Choose charting when discovery itself is the goal or measured chart rewards justify travel. Chart opportunities are shared and can disappear before arrival. Leaderboard overlap between credits and charts does not prove that charting caused those balances. [1] [8]

Optional: regional expansion

Inspect connected jump gates and construction requirements before moving capital. Construction consumes supplied cargo and is not documented here as a direct payment opportunity. Fund it for access only when the new route or exploration goal justifies it. [8]

Stop expansion purchases when their payback no longer fits your remaining usable hours. Finish worthwhile obligations, sell reachable inventory where profitable, and save the evidence needed for next cycle. Do not turn a weekly reset into a reason to fund an untested fleet.

Use a shared request budget for UI and workers. Official guidance lists 2 requests per second for IP and account limits, with burst handling. Follow actual retry headers and backoff. If an action result is uncertain, reconcile it before sending another write. [10] [12]

The objective tonight is to carry a tested procedure into the next cycle. Use the production site for live evidence. The development site and any practice environment have separate state. These steps are instructions for you; this report has executed no gameplay.

These are active-work time boxes, not promises about arrival or cooldown times. If travel runs longer, finish or safely pause the current test and defer the next. Two cycles are an initial check; repeat over different market conditions before scaling.

Record a useful trip receipt

Reset ID, ship and equipment, waypoints, start and finish times, cargo changes, extraction results, every purchase and sale total, fuel before and after, refuel cost, and interruptions. Also record when each market price was observed.

To test price decay, split an already justified sale into legal batches and refresh between them. To test probe visibility, read a market where only your probe is present. Neither test needs a capital purchase.

Use bounded execution

Start the local engine only when a preview needs execution. Starting it does not authorize gameplay. The app supports investigated Mining and Contracts runs, but this report does not claim autonomous trade routing or multi-ship hauling is ready. [12]

If using automation, approve a small duration, spending limit, reserve, and action budget. Stop on uncertainty, a changed world, unsafe fuel, or a missing buyer. Keep the PC awake for approved work and recheck after interruptions.

Interactive decision aid / illustrative inputs

Make each purchase earn its place

Buy only when added income from a ship, mount, or probe can repay its full setup cost well before reset. This calculator uses a conservative policy of payback within half the usable operating hours left. That fraction is a planning choice, not a server rule. Enter measured incremental income, after costs, compared with using your existing fleet.

Default numbers are fictional: 100,000 CR ÷ 5,000 added CR/hour = 20 operating hours to pay back. With 72 usable hours after setup, the policy cutoff is 36 hours. Total time from purchase is 22 hours including setup. Passing this check does not confirm affordability, demand, or future prices. Setup costs should include fuel and income displaced while positioning.
