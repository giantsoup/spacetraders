What we actually measured

One late-wipe hour earned 670,384 CR after fuel, with existing ships. Simple repetition would earn 112,624,512 CR in 168 hours before expansion spending. That is an arithmetic reference, not evidence the rate persists. We have no independent repeat runs or fleet-scale measurements.

Scaling stops helping when routes saturate, prices move, requests run out, or new ships cannot repay their cost. The model assumes profitable routes can be found up to its ceiling. It does not simulate individual markets, gate construction, exploration capital, outages as separate events, cargo losses, or a bad next-wipe layout. Actual earnings can fall below the cautious case.

Verification

Measured ledger identities, constant-rate control, zero-uptime control, and every-step cash conservation passed. Halving the decision interval changed the planning estimate by 0.10%. This verifies arithmetic and model consistency, not the assumptions.
