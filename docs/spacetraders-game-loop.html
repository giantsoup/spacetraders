02 / The repeatable pattern

The game loop is a set of decisions

Mining is one job inside this loop. Trading, contracts and exploration use the same habit of checking the situation before choosing another action.

  1. 01 →
    Observe

    Where is the ship? What is in its hold? How much fuel and money remain? What prices do you actually know?

  2. 02 →
    Choose a job

    Find a useful destination and a reason to go there. Budget the journey and the next operating costs.

  3. 03 →
    Send an instruction

    Travel, extract, sell or refuel. In this client, inspect a preview and approve the exact action separately.

  4. 04 →
    Wait when required

    Travel has an arrival time. Extraction creates a cooldown before another extraction. Waiting does not itself earn credits.

  5. 05 →
    Verify the result

    Read the confirmation and updated ship state. Did cargo, location, fuel and credits change as expected?

  6. 06 ↻
    Choose again

    Repeat a useful job, change the route, gather missing information, or stop. A completed trip is evidence for the next decision.

The loop returns to observation because the ship, market and your balance have changed.

05 / Different jobs, same decision loop

What are you trying to earn money from?

Mining

Extract → carry → sell

You produce cargo with a capable ship. The yield varies. Find a buyer for what you get, and cover fuel and other costs.

Another extraction requires cargo space and an expired cooldown. Your tutorial made one extraction to keep the first trip bounded.

Trading

Buy → carry → sell

You spend credits on goods at one market and sell them elsewhere. The price difference must cover the trip's costs.

Known prices, available cash and cargo space constrain the job. A large sale total can still hide a poor trade.

Contracts

Read terms → obtain goods → deliver → fulfill

A contract pays for specified work. Check the required good, quantity, destination, deadlines and payment terms before committing.

Delivering goods to a contract is different from selling them at a market. Keep required contract cargo until you decide how to fulfill the job.

Mining mechanics · Market mechanics · Contract terms · Delivery and fulfillment.

Some jobs earn information first

Scouting reveals useful places and prices. That information helps you choose future work, even when the scouting trip brings no immediate sale. A ship at a market can observe its detailed prices; recheck them before trading.

Our client currently supports manual travel, mining, cargo trades, refueling and bounded mining automation. Contracts, surveys, outfitting, repairs and intersystem travel remain later client work. The wider game offers more than this client currently exposes.

07 / Grow after you understand one job

A fleet repeats this loop in parallel

Each ship has its own location, cargo, fuel and task. All your ships share the agent's credits, so several useful jobs still need one affordable overall plan.

Learn a useful job →Observe its actual time, yield and costs.
Keep operating cash →Fund fuel and obligations before expanding.
Improve capacity if useful →Choose equipment or ships for a specific job.
Coordinate more jobs ↻Check each ship and the shared balance again.

Buying another ship is an optional strategic decision. Your first trip used the existing working miner. Autonomous play automates instructions and checks; it still needs useful routes and sensible limits.

08 / A version you can tell someone else

Explain the loop in half a minute

“In SpaceTraders, I run ships like a small space business. I check where they are and what jobs make sense, send instructions, wait for travel or cooldowns, then verify the results. A miner turns time into cargo; a market sale turns that cargo into credits. Fuel and other costs come out of those credits. I repeat useful jobs and change the plan when the results or prices change.”

Agent, ship, system and waypoint

The agent is your game identity and shared balance. A ship is one worker in your fleet. A system contains waypoints, which are individual places a ship can visit.

Orbit, dock and transit

Orbit is the ship state used for travel and mining. Docking allows market trades and refueling. Transit means it is travelling and has not arrived yet.

Fuel, cargo and credits

Fuel powers movement. Cargo is physical goods in one ship's hold. Credits are the agent's shared money. Extracting ore increases cargo; selling it increases credits.

Arrival and cooldown

Arrival says when travel should finish. A mining cooldown says when another extraction becomes possible. They are separate clocks, so a ship can navigate during an extraction cooldown.

Protected reserve

This client checks a minimum credit balance before local spending. It is a spending rule over the shared balance, not a separate stash of money.

Fresh quotes and transaction volume

A market listing names goods. A fresh observed quote supplies prices and a trade volume. This client limits each cargo trade to the currently quoted volume and checks the price bound again before sending it.
